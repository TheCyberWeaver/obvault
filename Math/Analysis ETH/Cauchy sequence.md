A sequence $(a_n)_{n\geq1}$ of [[Real numbers|real numbers]] is called a **Cauchy sequence** if its terms eventually become arbitrarily close to each other.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> $(a_n)_{n\geq1}$ is Cauchy if
> $$
> \forall{\varepsilon>0}\; \exists{N\in\mathbb{N}}\; \forall{n,m>N}:\; |a_n-a_m|<\varepsilon
> $$

The difference to ordinary convergence is that we do not assume the limit in advance. We only require that the sequence becomes internally consistent.

## Basic facts
Sequence is convergent in $\mathbb{R}$ $\Longleftrightarrow$ Sequence is Cauchy

## Example
The sequence $a_n=\frac{1}{n}$ is Cauchy because it converges to $0$.
