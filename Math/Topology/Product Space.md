## Universal properties of Product space
![[Pasted image 20260205183020.png]]

let $\Pi_{A}$ and $\Pi_{B}$ be projection maps.

Let $T$ be a arbitrary space that has maps $t_{A}$ and $t_{B}$ to $A$ and $B$, then there is a **unique** continuous map from $T$ to $A\times B$ defined by $\mathcal{U}:=(t_{A}(x),t_{B}(x))$ to make left and right commutes (universal property of product in the category of topological spaces)
That is $\Pi_{A}\circ \mathcal{U}=t_{A}$ and $\Pi_{B}\circ \mathcal{U}=t_{B}$
In fact, any set that satisfies this property (has a map that makes left and right commute), then it has to be isomorphic to the definition of $A\times B$

we want the projection function on the topology $A\times B$ to be continuous

## Definition
Let $X_{1},\dots,X_{n}$ be spaces. The product topology on $X_{1}\times\dots \times X_{n}$ is generated by the basis
$$
\mathcal{B}=\{ \mathcal{U}_{1}\times \dots \times \mathcal{U}_{n}|\mathcal{U}_{i} \text{ open in }X_{i} \;	\forall i \}
$$
## function family
given continuous maps $f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i}$
define $f_{1}\times\dots \times f_{n}:X_{1}\times\dots \times X_{n}\to Y_{1}\times\dots \times Y_{n}$
$$
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto, (f_{1}(x_{1}),\dots,f_{n}(x_{n}))
$$

a general indexed family of space can be notated as $\Pi _{\alpha \in A}X_{\alpha}$
## Characteristic Property
For any space $Y$ and function ${} f:Y\to \prod _{\alpha \in A} X_{\alpha} {}$ 
$f$ is continuous $\Longleftrightarrow$ $f_{\alpha}=\Pi_{\alpha} \circ f$ is continuous for all $\alpha \in A$
> [!Warning] 
> Note that $\Pi$ `\Pi` is different from $\prod$ `\prod`

We try to prove the $\Longleftarrow$ direction
### Proof using universal property
![[Pasted image 20260220212712.png]]
There exists a unique continuous map $\mathcal{U}:Y\to \prod _{\alpha \in A}X_{\alpha}$ that makes the graph commute
This gives $\Pi_{i}\circ \mathcal{U}=f_{\alpha}$ for all $\alpha \in A$
By assumption, $\Pi_{\alpha}\circ f=f_{a}$ for all $\alpha \in A$ 

Consider the underlying sets of these spaces, $\implies f=\mathcal{U}$ as function
Since $\mathcal{U}$ is continuous
$\implies$ $f$ is continuous


### Properties:
- If each $f_{i}$ is continuous, so is $f_{1}\times\dots \times f_{n}$
- If each $f_{i}$ is a [[Homeomorphism|homeomorphism]], so is $f_{1}\times\dots \times f_{n}$
